\noindent\textbf{Answer record --- (a)}\par
\begingroup\small
For the assignment architecture, use $V=50{,}257$, $T=1{,}024$, $L=48$, $D=1{,}600$, $H=25$, and $F=d_{\mathrm{ff}}=4{,}288$; hence the head dimension is $D/H=64$. The context length and head count affect computation but do not add trainable parameters.\par
\[
\begin{array}{lrl}
\text{Token embedding} & VD & =50{,}257(1{,}600)=80{,}411{,}200\\
\text{Attention per block} & 4D^2 & =4(1{,}600)^2=10{,}240{,}000\\
\text{SwiGLU per block} & 3DF & =3(1{,}600)(4{,}288)=20{,}582{,}400\\
\text{Two RMSNorms per block} & 2D & =3{,}200\\
\text{RoPE} & 0 & \text{trainable parameters}\\
\text{Final RMSNorm} & D & =1{,}600\\
\text{Untied LM head} & DV & =80{,}411{,}200
\end{array}
\]
Thus one block has $4D^2+3DF+2D=30{,}825{,}600$ parameters, and all 48 blocks have $1{,}479{,}628{,}800$.\par
\[
P=2VD+L(4D^2+3DF+2D)+D
=160{,}822{,}400+1{,}479{,}628{,}800+1{,}600
=\boxed{1{,}640{,}452{,}800}.
\]
The parameter shares are SwiGLU $60.22\%$, attention $29.96\%$, token embedding $4.90\%$, LM head $4.90\%$, and RMSNorm $0.01\%$; the feed-forward network has the largest share.\par
At four bytes per FP32 parameter, the weights occupy
\[
1{,}640{,}452{,}800\times4
=\boxed{6{,}561{,}811{,}200\ \mathrm{bytes}}
\approx\boxed{6.56\ \mathrm{GB}}
\approx 6.11\ \mathrm{GiB}.
\]
\textbf{One-to-two sentence deliverable:} The model has $1{,}640{,}452{,}800$ trainable parameters. Loading only its FP32 weights requires $6{,}561{,}811{,}200$ bytes (about $6.56$ GB or $6.11$ GiB), excluding gradients, activations, optimizer states, and other buffers.\par

\newpage
\noindent\textbf{(b) Matrix-multiplication FLOPs for one forward pass}\par
For a batch size of one, a $(m\times n)(n\times k)$ product costs $2mnk$ FLOPs. The following operations occur in each Transformer block at $T=1{,}024$:\par
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Matrix multiplication & FLOPs & GFLOPs per block\\
\hline
Query $XW_Q$ & $2TD^2$ & 5.243\\
Key $XW_K$ & $2TD^2$ & 5.243\\
Value $XW_V$ & $2TD^2$ & 5.243\\
Scores $QK^\top$ & $2T^2D$ & 3.355\\
Attention output $AV$ & $2T^2D$ & 3.355\\
Output projection $XW_O$ & $2TD^2$ & 5.243\\
SwiGLU $W_1$ & $2TDF$ & 14.051\\
SwiGLU $W_2$ & $2TDF$ & 14.051\\
SwiGLU $W_3$ & $2TDF$ & 14.051\\
\hline
Per block & $8TD^2+4T^2D+6TDF$ & 69.835\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The full model adds the LM-head product ($2TDV$), while embedding lookup, norms, RoPE, and activations are excluded from this matrix-multiplication count:\par
\[
C=L(8TD^2+4T^2D+6TDF)+2TDV
=\boxed{3{,}516{,}769{,}894{,}400}\ \text{FLOPs}
\approx 3.517\ \text{TFLOPs}.
\]
This counts dense $T\times T$ attention products, without halving them for the causal mask.\par
\medskip\noindent\textbf{(c) Largest FLOPs contributors}\par
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\hline
Component & TFLOPs & Share\\
\hline
SwiGLU FFN & 2.023 & 57.53\%\\
QKVO projections & 1.007 & 28.62\%\\
Attention scores and weighted values & 0.322 & 9.16\%\\
LM head & 0.165 & 4.68\%\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\textbf{One-to-two sentence deliverable:} SwiGLU FFNs dominate the forward-pass matrix-multiplication FLOPs, contributing about $57.53\%$. QKVO projections contribute $28.62\%$, while the quadratic attention products contribute $9.16\%$ at $T=1{,}024$.\par
\medskip\noindent\textbf{(d) GPT-2-sized model comparison}\par
With $V=50{,}257$ and $T=1{,}024$ fixed, $F$ is the nearest multiple of 64 to $(8/3)D$:\par
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\hline
Model & Layers & $D$ & Heads & $F$\\
\hline
Small & 12 & 768 & 12 & 2,048\\
Medium & 24 & 1,024 & 16 & 2,752\\
Large & 36 & 1,280 & 20 & 3,392\\
XL & 48 & 1,600 & 25 & 4,288\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\hline
Model & QKVO & Attention & FFN & LM head & Total TFLOPs\\
\hline
Small & 19.88\% & 13.25\% & 39.76\% & 27.10\% & 0.292\\
Medium & 24.83\% & 12.42\% & 50.05\% & 12.70\% & 0.830\\
Large & 27.32\% & 10.93\% & 54.30\% & 7.45\% & 1.769\\
XL & 28.62\% & 9.16\% & 57.53\% & 4.68\% & 3.517\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
At fixed $T$ and $V$, with $F\approx(8/3)D$, QKVO and FFN costs scale as $O(LD^2)$, attention-product cost as $O(LD)$, and LM-head cost as $O(D)$. Thus the QKVO and FFN shares rise as model width and depth increase, while the attention and LM-head shares fall.\par
\newpage
\noindent\textbf{(e) Increasing the XL context length to 16,384}\par
The QKVO, FFN, and LM-head terms are linear in $T$ and grow $16\times$; the attention-product term is quadratic in $T$ and grows $256\times$:\par
\[
C(T)=\underbrace{8LTD^2+6LTDF+2TDV}_{O(T)}
+\underbrace{4LT^2D}_{O(T^2)}.
\]
\[
C(16{,}384)=\boxed{133{,}577{,}729{,}638{,}400}\ \text{FLOPs}
\approx 133.578\ \text{TFLOPs},\qquad
\frac{C(16{,}384)}{C(1{,}024)}\approx\boxed{37.98}.
\]
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\hline
Component & $T=1{,}024$ & $T=16{,}384$\\
\hline
QKVO & 28.62\% & 12.06\%\\
Attention scores and weighted values & 9.16\% & 61.73\%\\
FFN & 57.53\% & 24.24\%\\
LM head & 4.68\% & 1.97\%\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\textbf{One-to-two sentence deliverable:} Increasing the context length from $1{,}024$ to $16{,}384$ raises the forward-pass FLOPs from about $3.517$ to $133.578$ TFLOPs, a factor of $37.98$. Attention products become dominant, rising from $9.16\%$ to $61.73\%$ because their cost is quadratic in sequence length.\par
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Supplementary crossover points (XL).}\par
Attention products alone exceed FFN FLOPs when $4T^2D>6TDF$, or $T>(3/2)F=6{,}432$. The full attention sublayer (QKVO plus attention products) exceeds FFN FLOPs when $8TD^2+4T^2D>6TDF$, or $T>(3/2)F-2D=3{,}232$.\par

\noindent\textbf{Answer record}\par
\begingroup\small
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrl}
\hline
Learning rate & Initial loss & Final loss & Observed behavior\\
\hline
$10$ & 24.91 & 0.01225 & Steady convergence\\
$100$ & 22.78 & $0$ & Faster convergence\\
$1{,}000$ & 29.30 & \texttt{inf} & Divergence\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For the toy objective $\mathcal L(W)=\frac{1}{100}\sum_{i,j}W_{ij}^{2}$ and the handout's learning-rate schedule, $\nabla_W\mathcal L=W/50$, so the update is
\[
W_{t+1}=\left(1-\frac{\eta}{50\sqrt{t+1}}\right)W_t.
\]
The first update multiplies $W_0$ by $0.8$, $-1$, or $-19$ for $\eta=10$, $100$, or $1{,}000$, respectively. For $\eta=100$, the fourth update has multiplier zero in exact arithmetic; for $\eta=1{,}000$, the first update multiplies loss by $19^2=361$, explaining the early growth and eventual observed overflow.\par
\textbf{One-to-two sentence deliverable:} With a learning rate of $10$, the loss decreases steadily toward zero; with $100$, it converges much faster for this particular objective. With $1{,}000$, updates overshoot the minimum, the loss grows rapidly, and the observed value eventually becomes \texttt{inf}.\par
